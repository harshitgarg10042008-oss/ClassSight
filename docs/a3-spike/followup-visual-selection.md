# A3 follow-up visual selection

Two real large-group candidates were inspected. The selected candidate is `W6TZmFqyQ1K0.jpg`, a 970 x 646 watermark-free lecture-hall photograph from the Freerange Stock search result. It visibly contains roughly 50–60 seated people, with many partly occluded heads and varied front/back row scales. It is closer to the classroom-distance problem than the prior two-person Obama/Biden photo, although it is still not a phone-camera back-of-classroom photo and most faces are viewed from behind, so identity matching will be limited.

The alternate crowd image `fh7QI7hCfnqd.jpg` is a 3,000 x 1,992 black-and-white audience scene with hundreds of mostly rear-view heads. It is useful as a detection stress case but is less suitable for a 2–4-person enrolled subset because visible frontal identity crops are scarce. The selected lecture-hall image will therefore be used as the larger stand-in, with the source/license documented as Freerange Stock for internal testing only.


## Exact Wikimedia alternatives inspected

The exact Wikimedia source page for [Students-Harlem-Community-Art-Center-1938.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Students-Harlem-Community-Art-Center-1938.jpg) identifies a 4,268 × 3,398 photograph by David Robbins / Works Progress Administration, Federal Art Project, and states that it is public domain as a U.S. federal government work. The displayed classroom scene has approximately a dozen visible children, so it is excellent for licensing but below the requested 20–30+ count.

The exact Wikimedia source page for [Grade school children posed in classroom, with teacher standing in back of room](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Grade_school_children_posed_in_classroom,_with_teacher_standing_in_back_of_room,_Washington,_D.C._LCCN96525653.jpg) identifies a 1,536 × 1,085 Library of Congress photograph by Frances Benjamin Johnston from 1899, with no known publication restrictions and public-domain markings. It visibly contains roughly 20–25 children and a teacher, with frontal faces, making it the best licensed candidate for an enrolled subset even though it is a posed historical classroom image rather than a modern phone-camera capture.
